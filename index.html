Profile Summary

I am a Research Assistant in the Department of Civil Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET), where I completed my B.Sc. in Civil Engineering in June 2025. I work on hydrological and groundwater modelling, stormwater management and climate change adaptation, with a focus on data-scarce, steep catchments.

Since November 2025 I have worked on a DRE-CUET funded project assessing groundwater, surface water and rainwater availability under climate change scenarios across the three hill districts of the Chattogram Hill Tracts. I am setting up SWAT+ for RCP 4.5 and 8.5, have mapped surface-water and groundwater potential zones with frequency-ratio analysis in ArcGIS, and have led field campaigns to springs, wells, households and district offices. I drafted our review of surface water–groundwater–rainwater interactions in the region, published in Hydrology (MDPI) in 2026.

My undergraduate thesis built and calibrated an EPA SWMM model of the Agrabad drainage network in Chattogram and redesigned its key conduits for extreme rainfall. I am now looking for a fully funded MSc or PhD position where I can combine process-based modelling, field data and machine learning to plan water supply under a changing climate.

Integrated Assessment of Groundwater, Surface Water and Rainwater Availability under Climate Change Scenarios in the Chattogram Hill Tracts (Integrated Water CHT)

Nov 2025 – Present
Research Assistant · PI: Prof. Dr. Aysha Akter · DRE-CUET funded (BDT 1.5M) · CUET/DRE/2025-26/CE/075
  • Modelling water availability under RCP 4.5 and 8.5 across 13,295 km² of Bandarban, Rangamati and Khagrachhari using SWAT+ with BWDB rainfall, SRTM DEM and Sentinel-1/2 data.

Undergraduate Thesis: Stormwater Drainage System Analysis and Design under Extreme Rainfall Events, Agrabad, Chittagong

Apr 2024 – Jun 2025
Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Aysha Akter · Grade A+ · Manuscript under review at Building and Environment
  • Built and calibrated an urban drainage model of the Agrabad catchment in EPA SWMM 5.2 (94 sub-catchments, 245 conduits, 186 junctions) against observed flows.
  • Simulated a recent observed storm and a 50-year design storm in one of Bangladesh's most flood-exposed commercial districts.
  • Redesigned 87 key conduits, cutting flooded junctions by 29.6% and peak flood volume by 39.9% under both storm scenarios.
